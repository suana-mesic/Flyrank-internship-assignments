## Why this matters

Once I know what I'm proving and the single action I want, the pages almost choose themselves: a place to land, the work that proves it, who I am, and how to get in touch. This assignment defines the few pages it takes to walk one visitor from landing, to trusting me, to that one action — and nothing more. Every page has to earn its place against my claim and my one action.

- **Claim:** Junior full-stack developer building functional, clean web applications in C# and TypeScript.
- **One person:** A hiring manager or technical lead at an established company.
- **One action:** Visit my GitHub.

## Pressure test

I ran one real prompt to stress-test the sitemap against my claim and my one action. Claude pushed back hard — the full prompt and response are in `pressure-test.png`.

**What I'll change based on it:**

1. **Stack signal above the fold.** Put C#, TypeScript and Angular in the Hero (and nav), so an ATS-trained or fast-scanning recruiter sees stack compatibility before deciding whether to keep reading.
2. **One dominant CTA on Contact.** Right now email, GitHub and LinkedIn carry equal weight, which means no priority. GitHub becomes the clear primary action; email and LinkedIn drop to secondary.
3. **Address the single-project risk (noted for Week 2).** The pressure test flagged that one case study is a risk — if a recruiter's need doesn't align with BookVerse, there's no fallback. I'll plan at least one more small project, and make sure the Work page shows decision-making, not a repackaged README.
